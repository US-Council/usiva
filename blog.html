The USIVA desk

The useful immigration question is usually earlier than the form.

These notes help readers slow down at the right moment: before a document is missing, before an activity is assumed to be authorized, and before a general promise gets mistaken for a personal answer.

Choose a reading pathOpen quick answers

Reading posture

Read broadly. Decide narrowly.

Field notes organize questions; they do not determine eligibility, authorize an activity, or replace a controlling source.

EDITORIAL NOTE
01

What belongs here

Planning content with a source trail.

USIVA field notes are not case studies, client testimonials, or predictions. They are short ways into questions about documentation, timing, status, work authorization, school coordination, and the point at which individualized advice becomes the responsible next step.

Read them as orientation. Then verify the current instruction with the school, sponsor, employer, government source, or licensed attorney that controls your situation.

    Scope

The source protocol

Use a public source to begin — not to skip the responsible authority.

  1. 01
    Find the primary source.

    Government pages, school instructions, employer guidance, and sponsor materials may be more current than a summary.

  2. 02
    Check the date and fit.

    A rule for one status, program, location, or activity may not answer another.

  3. 03
    Escalate the personal facts.

    General content cannot resolve a prior refusal, status concern, or individualized legal question.
