USIVA / services

Make the next immigration question visible before it becomes urgent.

USIVA is a visa-readiness and compliance-planning companion for individuals, families, employers, and organizations working through temporary or permanent immigration questions. The work starts with a clear file, a named decision, and the right person to verify it.

A service ledger

What careful planning can put in order.

Each move ends with a question for the authority that can actually confirm the answer.

  1. 01

    Build a readiness file

    Collect the identity, academic, funding, travel, and prior-visa records relevant to the decision in front of you.

    Verify with the school, program, sponsor, employer, or counsel named for your situation.

  2. 02

    Map the pathway

    Put stages, dates, owners, documents, and unresolved questions on one page so a missed handoff is easier to see.

    Keep current when a school instruction, government source, or program term changes.

  3. 03

    Name authorization boundaries

    Separate study, assistantship, research exposure, internship, travel, and employment instead of treating them as interchangeable.

    Ask first before beginning an activity that may require written authorization.

  4. 04

    Leave a source trail

    Save the public rule, school instruction, sponsor note, or professional advice that informed the next step, with the date checked.

    Escalate when the facts are personal, disputed, or too complex for general planning content.

The line that matters

Good planning never outruns authorization.

USIVA can help a reader frame the question. The university, designated school official, sponsor, employer, government agency, or licensed immigration attorney must provide the answer that controls the individual case.

01Before payment confirm who is providing what, under which terms, and whether the information is current.

02Before activity ask whether study, training, research, travel, or work needs written approval.

03Before filing check the current government and program instructions, then seek individualized advice when needed.

A useful next step

Bring one decision, not your entire life story.

Start with your current stage, the activity or filing you are considering, the date that matters, and the document or instruction you cannot interpret.
