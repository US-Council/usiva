Brief 01 / Classification desk

Purpose of travel is the first classification question

A visa category should follow the real purpose and activities of a trip, not a job title, a hopeful outcome, or a form someone mentioned in passing.

Audience
Readers deciding how to describe a proposed U.S. trip, study plan, research activity, or work question.
Last checked
2026-08-22
Reading path
/articles.html#purpose-of-travel-and-classification

Working thesis

Start with a plain-language activity inventory. Then compare that inventory with the current State Department category guidance and the agency or institution responsible for the underlying activity.

Carry forward

Write down what you will do, who directs it, who pays, where it happens, how long it lasts, and whether any part looks like study, training, or work before choosing a category to investigate.

In this brief

  1. 01Classify the activity, not the aspiration
  2. 02Build a purpose record that another reader can test
  3. 03Use category pages as lenses, not as a self-issued answer
  4. 04A visa is not the same thing as admission
  5. 05Escalate when the itinerary has a mixed purpose

The short version. Start with a plain-language activity inventory. Then compare that inventory with the current State Department category guidance and the agency or institution responsible for the underlying activity.

classify the activity

Classify the activity, not the aspiration

The State Department says that the purpose of intended travel and other facts determine what type of visa is required. That framing matters because one person can describe the same trip as a conference, a research visit, a training placement, or employment depending on which fact is emphasized. The classification question is about the complete activity, not the most flattering label.[1][3]

Make a private inventory before you search for a form: destination and dates; host or school; duties and deliverables; funding and compensation; supervision; whether you will enroll, train, perform services, or receive a benefit; and what you intend to do after the visit. This is an organizing tool, not a legal conclusion.[2]

Boundary note. Fact pattern first, category second. If an activity has mixed purposes, treat the mixed facts as the question to escalate rather than forcing them into the easiest label.

Source basis[1] Directory of Visa Categories[3] About Visas — The Basics

purpose record

Build a purpose record that another reader can test

A useful purpose record is short enough to hand to a school, consular post, sponsor, employer, or attorney and specific enough to expose the unresolved issue.

  1. 01

    One sentence

    State the primary reason for travel without using a visa class as a substitute for the description.

  2. 02

    Activity list

    List each planned activity, including meetings, classes, laboratory work, observation, training, services, paid work, and unpaid work.

  3. 03

    Control and funding

    Name who directs the work, who supplies the location or equipment, who pays expenses or compensation, and what records support those facts.

  4. 04

    Duration and exit

    Record the planned dates, locations, and what happens at the end of the visit or program; do not turn a general intention into a guarantee.

  5. 05

    Verification owner

    Assign the next question to the relevant official agency, school, sponsor, employer representative, or qualified immigration attorney.

Source basis[1] Directory of Visa Categories[2] U.S. Visas — overview and Visa Wizard

visa is not admission

A visa is not the same thing as admission

A visa is a travel document and category decision handled through the State Department’s visa process. At the port of entry, CBP conducts inspection and determines whether an arriving applicant is admissible under the facts and law applicable at that time. The State Department and CBP pages should therefore be read together when the question is travel, not treated as interchangeable approval screens.[2][7]

Keep the distinction visible in your record: visa category, visa validity, admission inspection, admission record, and the activity you intend to perform are related but separate questions. A category page cannot answer every question about a particular person’s history or itinerary.[7]

Boundary note. Verify current travel, document, and admission instructions with the relevant embassy or consulate and CBP source before making arrangements.

Source basis[2] U.S. Visas — overview and Visa Wizard[7] Admission into the United States

escalate mixed purpose

Escalate when the itinerary has a mixed purpose

The right next action is not to pick the least restrictive-sounding category. Preserve the complete activity description and ask the authority responsible for the program, admission, visa, status, or employment question to verify the classification.[1][7]

  • A visitor trip also includes hands-on work, services, or compensation.
  • A research or academic relationship is described as informal, but another organization directs the duties or pays the person.
  • A school, sponsor, employer, or consular post gives instructions that do not match a general web summary.
  • The proposed activity depends on prior immigration history, a previous refusal, a change of status, or a personal deadline.

Source basis[1] Directory of Visa Categories[7] Admission into the United States

Brief 02 / Route map

Immigrant or nonimmigrant? Draw the route before the form

The first fork is whether the plan is temporary or intended to lead to permanent residence. From there, location, petition requirements, and the responsible agency change the route.

Audience
Readers comparing a temporary visit, study or work plan with a permanent family- or employment-based immigration objective.
Last checked
2026-08-22
Reading path
/articles.html#immigrant-vs-nonimmigrant-route-map

Working thesis

Route mapping prevents a common category error: treating a petition, a visa, an admission decision, an immigration status, and a green-card process as one event.

Carry forward

Make a one-page route map with the goal, location, petitioner or sponsor, next agency, record number, and unresolved question at each handoff.

In this brief

  1. 01Two route families, two different planning questions
  2. 02A route map with the handoffs left visible
  3. 03Approval at one desk is not completion at every desk
  4. 04A five-line route worksheet
  5. 05Facts that can change the map

The short version. Route mapping prevents a common category error: treating a petition, a visa, an admission decision, an immigration status, and a green-card process as one event.

two families

Two route families, two different planning questions

Nonimmigrant routes are generally organized around a temporary purpose: visiting, studying, exchanging, working in a temporary classification, or another defined activity. Immigrant routes are organized around the objective of immigrating and living permanently, with family, employment, adoption, special immigrant, diversity, and other category families listed by the State Department.[1][3]

The words temporary and permanent describe the route objective, not a promise about how a person feels about the future. A reader should document the actual purpose, the category-specific requirements, and the relevant agency’s current instructions rather than infer a route from a hoped-for long-term outcome.[1]

Source basis[1] Directory of Visa Categories[3] Immigrate to the United States

approval is not admission

Approval at one desk is not completion at every desk

USCIS may adjudicate a petition or benefit request. The State Department may process a visa application through a consular post or the National Visa Center for certain immigrant cases. CBP may then inspect an arriving applicant for admission. These decisions can be connected in a route, but each has its own record, authority, and current instructions.[5]

Use precise verbs in your notes: filed, received, approved, transferred, documentarily complete, scheduled, issued, admitted, or denied. Avoid writing “approved” when the notice only says a filing was received, and avoid writing “visa approved” when the available record is a petition approval or an admission inspection.[5][6]

Source basis[5] National Visa Center[6] Admission into the United States

map worksheet

A five-line route worksheet

  1. 01

    Goal

    Write the immediate objective: visit, study, exchange, temporary work, or permanent immigration.

  2. 02

    Location

    Record whether the person is outside the United States, inside the United States, or planning travel between stages.

  3. 03

    Sponsor or petitioner

    Name the school, exchange sponsor, employer, family petitioner, or other responsible party.

  4. 04

    Agency record

    Identify the notice, petition, application, case number, visa record, admission record, or school record that controls the next step.

  5. 05

    Open question

    Write one unresolved question and assign it to the relevant agency, school, sponsor, employer, or qualified attorney.

Source basis[2] U.S. Visas — overview and Visa Wizard[4] Green Card Processes and Procedures[5] National Visa Center

map changes

Facts that can change the map

When one of these facts is present, keep the map and the source note together. Verify current requirements with the agency that owns the next decision instead of relying on a route diagram as if it were a determination.[1][5]

  • The person’s physical location and whether the plan uses consular processing or an in-country process.
  • Whether a petitioner, employer, school, or designated sponsor is required for the category.
  • Whether the category is subject to a numerical limit or another availability rule.
  • Prior immigration history, refusals, status issues, travel plans, dependents, or changes in the proposed activity.
  • The difference between a public information page and instructions issued to the person on a case-specific notice.

Source basis[1] Directory of Visa Categories[5] National Visa Center

Brief 03 / Agency desk map

USCIS, State, and CBP are different desks

The immigration system is easier to read when each agency’s decision is named separately: benefit or petition, visa processing, and inspection/admission are not interchangeable.

Audience
Readers unsure which federal agency, portal, notice, or official source should receive the next question.
Last checked
2026-08-22
Reading path

Working thesis

A clean handoff starts by naming the decision being requested and the agency that has authority over it.

Carry forward

Before you ask for an update, write the agency, decision type, record number, current status, and exact question in one line.

In this brief

  1. 01One journey can contain several different decisions
  2. 02The desk map

The short version. A clean handoff starts by naming the decision being requested and the agency that has authority over it.

different decisions

One journey can contain several different decisions

USCIS, the Department of State, and CBP all sit inside a larger federal system, but they do not answer the same question. USCIS handles immigration benefit and naturalization services and related adjudications. The State Department handles visa information and consular processing. CBP inspects people seeking admission at ports of entry and makes the admission decision under its authority.[1][2][4][5]

The practical result is that a person may have a USCIS notice, a State Department visa record, and a CBP admission record at different moments. One record can inform the next handoff without replacing it.[3][6]

Source basis[1] About USCIS[2] U.S. Visas — overview and Visa Wizard[5] Admission into the United States

common collisions

Three collisions that create bad handoffs

A reliable note names both the event and the source: “USCIS notice says…,” “State portal shows…,” “CBP I-94 record shows…,” or “employer’s Form I-9 process requires….” That language keeps an administrative record from becoming a larger conclusion.[3][6]

  • Petition approval is recorded as if it were visa issuance. The next step may still belong to the State Department or a consular post.
  • Visa validity is recorded as if it were an admission decision. CBP conducts inspection and maintains the relevant admission record.
  • Employer verification is treated as if it decides the underlying immigration classification. Form I-9 and E-Verify are employment-verification processes with their own rules.

Source basis[3] National Visa Center[6] I-94 Official Website

handoff packet

Build a handoff packet that respects each desk

  1. 01

    Decision

    State what you need: classification, benefit status, visa-process update, admission record, school instruction, or employment verification.

  2. 02

    Record

    Identify the notice, receipt, case number, passport/visa record, I-94 record, school record, or employer document that supports the question.

  3. 03

    Timeline

    List only the dates that can be supported by records, and label them as issue date, submission date, transfer date, travel date, or requested action date.

  4. 04

    Question

    Ask one concrete question of the agency or institution that controls the next step; do not send a broad request to every desk.

  5. 05

    Privacy

    Use the official secure channel and share identifiers only after verifying the domain and recipient.

Source basis[3] National Visa Center[6] I-94 Official Website

agency caveat

The source of truth is the authority for that decision

USIVA can help a reader organize the question and find the public starting point. It cannot transfer authority from USCIS, the State Department, CBP, a school, a sponsor, an employer, or a qualified attorney. Verify current requirements with the responsible official source before filing, traveling, working, or relying on a deadline.[1][2][5]

Boundary note. USIVA is not a law firm. If personal facts could change eligibility or consequences, stop at orientation and obtain individualized advice from the appropriate professional or agency.

Source basis[1] About USCIS[2] U.S. Visas — overview and Visa Wizard[5] Admission into the United States

Brief 04 / Evidence desk

Evidence is a chain of custody for your own facts

A readiness file is useful when every important claim has a source, date, owner, and question attached to it—not when it is merely large.

Audience
Students, researchers, families, and employers organizing records before a school, agency, sponsor, or attorney review.
Last checked
2026-08-22
Reading path
/articles.html#record-readiness

Working thesis

Treat evidence as a traceable record of decisions. Separate what a document proves from what you still need someone with authority to decide.

Carry forward

For each record, write: who issued it, when it was issued, when you checked it, what it supports, what it does not establish, and who owns the next answer.

In this brief

  1. 01A document answers one question at a time
  2. 02Use an evidence matrix before you make a packet
  3. 03Version control is part of evidence quality
  4. 04A readiness test for the next reviewer
  5. 05Privacy and escalation are evidence decisions too

The short version. Treat evidence as a traceable record of decisions. Separate what a document proves from what you still need someone with authority to decide.

one document one question

A document answers one question at a time

A passport can support identity and travel-document facts. An admission letter can support a school relationship. A financial record can support a source and date of funds. A role description can explain duties. None of those records, standing alone, answers every question about a person’s eligibility, status, authorization, admissibility, or future outcome.[1][4][3]

The USCIS Policy Manual and official State Department pages are useful for understanding the public framework, but a general framework is not a universal evidence list. The school, consular post, agency notice, employer process, or qualified attorney may require a different or more specific record for the facts at hand.[3][2]

Source basis[3] USCIS Policy Manual[1] Student Visa[2] Directory of Visa Categories

version control

Version control is part of evidence quality

Living records change. Keep the history instead of silently replacing the evidence that informed an earlier decision.

  1. 01

    Name

    Give the record a precise name that distinguishes a notice, letter, form version, portal screenshot, or instruction page.

  2. 02

    Date

    Record the issue date, the date you retrieved it, and the last-checked date for the official page or tool.

  3. 03

    Owner

    Identify who can issue, correct, interpret, or replace the record.

  4. 04

    Meaning

    Write what the record supports and what it cannot establish. Mark an assumption as an assumption.

  5. 05

    Change note

    When the record changes, retain the old note and describe what changed, who confirmed it, and what action follows.

Source basis[3] USCIS Policy Manual[1] Student Visa

readiness test

A readiness test for the next reviewer

If the answer is no, improve the index before adding more documents. A shorter, traceable packet is often a better starting point for the authority that must answer the question.[3]

  • Can the reviewer tell why this record belongs in the packet?
  • Can the reviewer find the issuing authority and verify the current version?
  • Can the reviewer see which dates are confirmed and which are projected?
  • Can the reviewer distinguish a school, sponsor, employer, agency, or personal statement?
  • Can the reviewer identify the one question that remains open?
  • Can the reviewer understand the packet without receiving unnecessary passport, financial, or immigration identifiers?

Source basis[3] USCIS Policy Manual

privacy and escalation

Privacy and escalation are evidence decisions too

Keep passport scans, immigration identifiers, financial records, employer-sensitive material, and private correspondence in the secure system specified by the school, agency, employer, sponsor, or attorney. Public articles and shared links are not secure document-transfer channels.

Stop relying on a general checklist when a decision turns on a prior refusal, status concern, unauthorized work, criminal or civil issue, complex travel history, dependent, disputed employer relationship, or personal deadline. Preserve the chronology and take it to the agency, institution, or qualified attorney responsible for the answer.[3][4]

Boundary note. Verify current evidence instructions with the relevant official agency before filing or submitting records. USIVA is not a law firm and cannot decide what a particular case requires.

Brief 05 / Workplace desk

Employer orientation: Form I-9, E-Verify, and the authorization record

Form I-9 and E-Verify are employer-side verification processes. They should be coordinated with the worker’s actual immigration and work-authorization record, not used as a shortcut around it.

Employers, HR teams, international workers, students, and researchers preparing for a compliant hiring handoff.

Start with the employer’s Form I-9 duty

USCIS’s I-9 Central is the primary employer resource for Form I-9, employment-eligibility verification, and current employer guidance. The employer should use the current form, instructions, and official handbook resources rather than an old checklist or a document list copied into a job email.[1][2]

  1. 01

    Role facts

    Record the legal employer, worksite or arrangement, job title, duties, start conditions, and who supervises the work.

  2. 02

worker side

For the worker, verification is not the whole authorization analysis

A completed Form I-9 or an E-Verify result does not, by itself, answer whether a proposed role fits the worker’s underlying immigration classification, school or sponsor rules, authorization period, or specific duties. Those questions may belong to USCIS, a DSO or sponsor, an employer representative, or qualified immigration counsel.[1][4][5]

Before starting or changing a role, keep the offer or role description, duties, location, supervisor, compensation, start date, and any authorization record together. Ask the authority responsible for the status or program to verify the activity before relying on the employer’s onboarding step as permission to work.[1][5]

Source basis[1] I-9 Central[5] USCIS Policy Manual

clean hiring file

What a clean hiring handoff contains

  • A role description that matches the work the person will actually perform.
  • The employer’s current Form I-9 and E-Verify operating instructions, owned by the authorized HR or compliance team.
  • A private record of the worker’s status or authorization source, dates, and any school or sponsor instruction that controls the activity.
  • A written owner for a mismatch, expiring document, changed duty, changed worksite, or uncertain classification.
  • A secure channel for any personal record; no passport scan or immigration identifier in an open email thread unless the authorized recipient requires it.
Boundary note. Verify current requirements with USCIS, E-Verify, the employer’s authorized representative, the school or sponsor, and qualified counsel where the facts require legal analysis. USIVA is not a law firm.

statuses can differ

Why two official statuses can both be true

A USCIS account may show a case history while the case-status tool shows a shorter public status line. A State Department case may move from USCIS to the NVC or a consular post. A visa record can exist before travel, while CBP creates or updates the admission record during inspection. These systems describe different events and should not be forced into one timeline label.[2][6][5][7]

Write the event exactly as the source presents it and attach the source URL, date retrieved, and record owner. If the status line appears to conflict with a notice or admission record, preserve both and ask the agency that owns the disputed event to reconcile them.[3]

Source basis[2] USCIS online account[6] National Visa Center[5] Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC)[7] I-94 Official Website

account security

Account security is part of the handoff

  • Use the official agency domain and confirm the address before entering a receipt number, case number, password, or personal identifier.
  • Use an individual account when the agency says the account is personal; do not share credentials as a team workaround.
  • Keep official notices and account messages in the secure location the agency or authorized representative specifies.
  • Treat a third-party tracker, social post, or email forward as a lead only; verify the underlying status in the official system.

Source basis[2] USCIS online account[3] USCIS Contact Center[6] National Visa Center

status escalation

When a status page is not enough

Escalate when the question depends on a prior refusal, a status or travel consequence, a personal deadline, a missing or returned document, a changed address, a changed employer or role, a dependent, or any fact that the public status line does not show. The right escalation is the official agency contact path, school or sponsor owner, employer representative, or qualified attorney—not a stronger guess.[3][6]

Brief 07 / Data desk

A small research protocol for official immigration data

Good immigration research begins with definitions, release context, and source ownership. This brief gives readers a repeatable way to use DHS, Data.gov, USCIS, and State Department data without manufacturing a headline.

Audience
Researchers, policy readers, journalists, students, and practitioners who need a careful starting protocol for public immigration data.
Last checked
2026-08-22
Reading path
/articles.html#official-immigration-data-research

Working thesis

Define the event, unit, period, source, denominator, and caveat before reading a number. The official dataset or report remains the authority, and the catalog is not the same thing as the underlying data.

Carry forward

Create a research card for every result: question, official source URL, publication or dataset title, release date, filters, measure, denominator, retrieval date, and limitations.

  1. 01Define the event before counting it
  2. 02What each official source is for
  3. 03Four distinctions that prevent bad conclusions
  4. 04Use a reproducible research card
  5. 05A catalog record is not automatically a public data file

The short version. Define the event, unit, period, source, denominator, and caveat before reading a number. The official dataset or report remains the authority, and the catalog is not the same thing as the underlying data.

define before counting

Define the event before counting it

“Immigration data” is not one measure. A table may describe visa issuances, admissions, lawful permanent resident status, applications received, approvals, denials, pending cases, enforcement actions, or another event. Those events involve different agencies, records, and denominators. Start by naming exactly what the table measures.[1][4][3]

DHS’s Yearbook of Immigration Statistics, the USCIS Data Library, and the State Department’s visa-statistics pages each organize their own data products. Read the methodology, table title, notes, fiscal-year label, and publication context before combining results across them.[1][3][4]

Source basis[1] Yearbook of Immigration Statistics[3] Immigration and Citizenship Data[4] Visa Statistics

four distinctions

Four distinctions that prevent bad conclusions

The State Department’s visa-statistics page explicitly warns that monthly reports contain preliminary data subject to change. Data.gov likewise explains that catalog metadata and the underlying agency data have different owners and update paths. These are not footnotes to hide; they determine how much a result can claim.[4][6]

  • Fiscal year versus calendar year: write the period exactly as the source defines it.
  • Issuance, admission, adjustment, approval, receipt, and pending: these are not interchangeable events or people counts.
  • Counts versus unique people: one person, case, petition, application, or admission can appear in different measures under different identifiers or periods.
  • Preliminary versus revised data: preserve the release date and check the live official page before publication or reuse.

Source basis[4] Visa Statistics[6] Data.gov

research card

Use a reproducible research card

  1. 01

    Question

    Write the question as an observable event, not a conclusion: “How many reported issuances…?” or “What does this USCIS table measure?”

  2. 02

    Source

    Record the official publisher, exact report or dataset title, URL, table/resource identifier, and the date last checked.

  3. 03

    Scope

    Write the period, geography, category, filters, inclusion rules, and whether the result is preliminary, final, or revised.

  4. 04

    Measure

    Name the unit: cases, applications, petitions, people, visas, admissions, actions, or another unit defined by the source.

  5. 05

    Limit

    Record what the source cannot answer and what another official source would be needed to complete the picture.

  6. 06

    Recheck

    Before publication, reopen the live official page and confirm that the resource, definition, and release context have not changed.

Source basis[1] Yearbook of Immigration Statistics[3] Immigration and Citizenship Data[4] Visa Statistics[7] Data.gov Catalog

catalog is not data

A catalog record is not automatically a public data file

Data.gov describes itself as a metadata catalog: it points readers to datasets and their publishing agencies, but it does not own or manage the underlying data. A catalog record can also identify an access level, a contact, a resource URL, and a metadata update date that differ from the data’s own release date.[6][7]

Use a Data.gov record to locate and document a source. Then follow the publishing agency’s resource and read its access, privacy, methodology, and license information. If the record is not public or the file cannot be verified, say so and do not fill the gap with an estimate.[8][6]

Source basis[6] Data.gov[7] Data.gov Catalog[8] Data.gov DHS CIS2 catalog record

no personal forecast

Do not turn aggregate data into a personal forecast

A published count can describe the source’s defined workload or event. It cannot, by itself, predict whether a particular person will receive a visa, be admitted, obtain a benefit, receive work authorization, or have a case decided by a certain date. Those questions depend on the person’s facts, category, record, current instructions, and responsible agency.[1][3][4]

This brief intentionally does not reproduce a headline statistic. It gives the reader a method for finding the current official table and preserving its limitations. Verify the live DHS, USCIS, State Department, and Data.gov pages before relying on a research result.[2][3][4][6]

Boundary note. USIVA is not a law firm and is not a statistical agency. For legal decisions, use the relevant official agency or qualified immigration counsel; for data questions, contact the publishing agency named in the official record.

Source basis[2] Immigration Statistics[3] Immigration and Citizenship Data[4] Visa Statistics[6] Data.gov
