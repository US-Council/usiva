USIVA / tips and FAQ

Questions worth asking before you act.

Use these answers to frame a visa or immigration question, identify the source that owns the next answer, and recognize when general information is not enough.

Common questions

Start with the answer, then open the source.

These responses describe a planning posture. They are not legal advice, a government decision, or a promise that any category or status is available.

RecordsAuthorizationEscalation
What is a readiness file?

A private working set of identity, academic, funding, travel, prior-visa, family, employment, or program records relevant to the decision in front of you. Mark what is known, missing, dated, and still awaiting verification.

Does a complete file mean I am eligible?

No. A well-organized file can make a handoff clearer, but it cannot determine eligibility, admissibility, work authorization, visa issuance, or government approval.

Who should answer a university or status question?

Start with the written instructions for the program and the designated school official or sponsor named for the situation. Questions about personal history, status, or legal interpretation may require qualified immigration counsel.

Are study, research, training, travel, and employment the same activity?

No. They may involve different permissions, documents, and timing. Describe the actual activity, who directs it, whether compensation is involved, and whether written authorization is required before it begins.

Can I begin work while I wait for an answer?

Do not assume that submitting a form, receiving an invitation, or being interested in a role authorizes activity. Ask the responsible school, sponsor, government source, employer, or attorney before beginning anything that may be training, research, or employment.

Does USIVA issue a visa or guarantee an outcome?

No. USIVA is not a law firm and does not provide legal representation, file applications, decide eligibility, or guarantee admission, employment, funding, visa issuance, work authorization, or government approval.

Why do sources and dates matter?

Rules, fees, school instructions, sponsor requirements, and program details can change. A source that applies to one status, institution, location, or date may not answer another question. Save the source and the date you checked it.

When is general content not enough?

When the answer depends on a prior refusal, status concern, criminal or immigration history, a complex work arrangement, a change of status, or another personal fact a public page cannot evaluate. Prepare the facts and use the responsible authority, including licensed counsel when appropriate.

Should I send sensitive documents through this site?

No. Do not submit passwords, passport numbers, receipt numbers, identity documents, or private case records through this static site. Use the verified channel requested by the responsible agency, school, employer, sponsor, or counsel.
